## 1. What CRA says (Line 34990)

CRA's dedicated page "Line 34990 – Top-up tax credit" states:

- The TTC "effectively maintains a 15% rate for certain non-refundable tax credits claimed on
  amounts over the first income tax bracket threshold of $57,375 for 2025."
- You may claim it "if you are claiming certain non-refundable tax credits that are affected by the
  reduction to the lowest marginal individual income tax rate from 15% to 14.5% for 2025."
- The calculation is done via the chart for line 34990 on the Federal Worksheet.

Source: https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/topics/about-your-tax-return/tax-return/completing-a-tax-return/deductions-credits-expenses/line-34990-top-up-tax-credit.html

## 2. Policy origin — Budget 2025 and the rate cut

- The rate cut itself (lowest bracket 15% → 14.5% for 2025, 14% for 2026+) was enacted by
  **Bill C-4 (45-1)**, long title *An Act respecting certain affordability measures for Canadians
  and another measure*, short title *Making Life More Affordable for Canadians Act*.
  Royal Assent **2026-03-12**; published as **S.C. 2026, c. 2**.
  - LEGISinfo: https://www.parl.ca/legisinfo/en/bill/45-1/c-4
  - Royal Assent text: https://www.parl.ca/DocumentViewer/en/45-1/bill/C-4/royal-assent
  - Finance news release: https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/news/2026/03/legislation-to-make-life-more-affordable-receives-royal-assent.html
- **Bill C-4 does NOT contain the top-up tax credit.** The TTC was proposed separately in
  **Budget 2025** (tabled 2025-11-04) to prevent taxpayers with large non-refundable credit amounts
  (e.g. one-time tuition or medical claims) from being made worse off by the rate cut, "for taxation
  years from 2025 to 2030."
  - Miller Thomson Budget 2025 summary: https://www.millerthomson.com/en/insights/federal-budget-review/2025-canada-federal-budget-a-summary-of-key-tax-proposals/
  - Finance report on the credit-value impact of the rate cut: https://www.canada.ca/en/department-finance/services/publications/report-impact-reducing-lowest-marginal-personal-income-tax-rate-non-refundable-tax-credits.html
  - Advisor.ca: https://www.advisor.ca/industry-news/industry/top-up-tax-credit-helps-taxpayers-with-significant-tax-credits-amid-middle-class-tax-cut/
    (describes the TTC as "intended to ensure individuals don't have a higher tax liability if
    Bill C-4 is enacted" — i.e. the TTC rides in a different bill than C-4).

## 4. Operative mechanics (for engine implementation)

- Temporary, non-refundable, transitional credit, **2025–2030 taxation years only**.
- Applies when an individual's total qualifying non-refundable tax credit *amounts* (the base
  amounts, not the credit value) exceed the first-bracket threshold ($57,375 for TY2025;
  $58,523 for 2026 per RBC/Advisor coverage — index annually).
- Effect: the excess of qualifying credit amounts over the threshold generates credit at 15%
  instead of the reduced lowest rate. Equivalently, the top-up equals the excess multiplied by the
  gap between 15% and the lowest rate for the year.
  - **Derived, not directly sourced:** for TY2025 the blended lowest rate is 14.5%, so the top-up
    rate on the excess is 0.5%; for 2026–2030 (lowest rate 14%) it is 1%. This arithmetic follows
    from the cited descriptions ("effectively maintains a 15% rate") but the exact statutory formula
    was not readable — implement from the CRA Federal Worksheet chart for line 34990 and treat the
    Worksheet as the fixture oracle.
- Sources for mechanics: CRA Line 34990 page (above); Advisor.ca tracker (above); Seniuk & Marcato:
  https://seniuk.com/new-top-up-tax-credit-for-2025-why-cra-introduced-it-and-who-it-helps/
